Research

Green modification of cellulose, chitin and lignin

Selective chemistry in water or without solvents that changes just enough of a biopolymer to unlock a new function.

Cellulose, chitin and lignin carry functional groups that can be addressed selectively. We develop modification routes that follow green-chemistry principles – for example esterification of never-dried cellulose in water, solvent-free acylation, or reversible modifications that can be removed again after processing to recover the native structure. Close analytical follow-up of each reaction connects the degree and location of modification with the properties of the final material.

Projects

  • SusFoMaSustainable transformation of food loss into processable materials

    Agricultural residues such as maize straw and sugarcane bagasse are selectively esterified into mouldable, nanofibre-reinforced bioplastics – keeping the cellulose nanofibres intact.

    Funded by Gesellschaft für Forschungsförderung Niederösterreich (GFF NÖ), grant FTI23-G-019
    Jun 2024 – Dec 2027

  • TrenDeCoEnzymatically recyclable, sustainable 3D-printable composites for the circular economy

    Bio-based composites for high-resolution stereolithography 3D printing that can be broken down chemically or enzymatically after use – "degradation on demand".

    Funded by Gesellschaft für Forschungsförderung Niederösterreich (GFF NÖ)
    Jun 2026 – Jun 2029
