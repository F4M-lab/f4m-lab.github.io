Future Feedstocks for Functional Materials

We turn underused biomass and waste streams into functional, high-performance materials.

FeedstockFunctional material

The F4M group develops selective, resource-efficient chemical and physical routes that turn overlooked biomass – aquatic plants and reed, straw and bagasse, insect farming residues – into functional materials. We work with cellulose, chitin, proteins and lignin, from green modification chemistry to nanofibrils, hydrogels, aerogels, bioplastics and 3D-printable composites, and we link molecular design to the properties of the final material.

Research

All research areas

  • Underused biomass and decentralised biorefineries

    Aquatic plants, reed, straw, bagasse and other residues as starting points for materials – processed close to where they arise.

    MBAWBDMCNT

  • Green modification of cellulose, chitin and lignin

    Selective chemistry in water or without solvents that changes just enough of a biopolymer to unlock a new function.

    MBNTSZ

  • Nanocellulose and nanochitin materials

    Nanofibrils, soft nanoparticles, hydrogels, aerogels, films and nanopapers assembled from bio-based building blocks.

    MBPYLP

  • Chitin–protein composites from insects

    Insect farming produces chitin and protein side streams – we tune their ratio to tailor the material properties.

    MBPY

  • Bioplastics, 3D printing and biomedical materials

    From nanofibre-reinforced thermoplastics and recyclable 3D-printing resins to packaging, food trays and scaffolds for cell culture.

    MBAWBDMCNTLPSZ

Projects

All projects
  • InnoWAPInnovative valorisation of aquatic plants from the Danube region in a decentralised biorefinery

    Aquatic plants from the Alte and Neue Donau in Vienna and reed from Lake Neusiedl are turned into specialty papers, textile fibres, food trays and packaging – processed close to where they are harvested.

    Funded by Austrian Research Promotion Agency (FFG)
    Mar 2025 – Aug 2028

  • SusFoMaSustainable transformation of food loss into processable materials

    Agricultural residues such as maize straw and sugarcane bagasse are selectively esterified into mouldable, nanofibre-reinforced bioplastics – keeping the cellulose nanofibres intact.

    Funded by Gesellschaft für Forschungsförderung Niederösterreich (GFF NÖ), grant FTI23-G-019
    Jun 2024 – Dec 2027

  • TrenDeCoEnzymatically recyclable, sustainable 3D-printable composites for the circular economy

    Bio-based composites for high-resolution stereolithography 3D printing that can be broken down chemically or enzymatically after use – "degradation on demand".

    Funded by Gesellschaft für Forschungsförderung Niederösterreich (GFF NÖ)
    Jun 2026 – Jun 2029

  • Nanocellulose from Brazilian biodiversity as biomedical scaffolds for 3D cell culture

    Nanocellulose aerogels from the native Brazilian fibres curauá and piaçaba as scaffolds for neural cell culture – an Austrian–Brazilian cooperation.

    Funded by OeAD – Austria's Agency for Education and Internationalisation
    Jan 2026 – Dec 2027
