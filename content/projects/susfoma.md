Straw and other lignocellulosic residues are produced in enormous quantities, yet turning them into strong, shapeable materials remains difficult. SusFoMa tests the hypothesis that these residues can be converted directly into precursors of cellulose-nanofibre-reinforced thermoplastics. A controlled esterification selectively modifies the biopolymer matrix of local food residues while the cellulose nanofibres – the strongest structural units of the plant cell wall – stay intact. The aim is a competitive, high-performance bioplastic that can replace fossil-based, non-degradable plastics.
